# Digest — what the surviving books are (2026-09-09)

A high-level characterisation of the fifteen books that survive the owner's filter set, drawn from Addenda 22–28. Deck R8h, basis (B): 3% real draw from the actual accounts, Social Security offsetting from year 11, federal and Missouri tax, 40 years, 240k engine paths.

## What "surviving" means
A book survives if it passes the full gate battery on the current deck, holds no more than 20% in grade-C rows, keeps the nuclear complex under 38% of book variance and any one holding under 24%, and is not a copy of another survivor (fewer than 3% of the portfolio would move). Of 51 candidate books, 36 fell: fourteen on nuclear concentration — the previous recommendation R7a and every variant of it — eight on gates, including the two highest-wealth books R5c and R5c2, seven on grade-C weight, five as duplicates, and two on single-holding concentration. Those two were the starting book P1 and its one-trade variant P2.

## One family, one dial
The fifteen are not fifteen ideas. Every one holds the same core — GLDM 15, SHLD 15, CIBR 13–15, CME 5, MSCI 5, BRKB 5 — and eleven of the fifteen hold FNV 4–5. What varies is a single dial: how much managed-futures ballast (DBMF 5 → 15, sometimes plus CTA or KMLM) is exchanged for thematic exposure (NUKZ 6 → 11, URNM 3 → 8, XAR, SETM). Turning that dial toward thematics buys expected wealth and sells reliability, and nothing else of substance changes.

Expected wealth is the log-average terminal multiple of the initial corpus in today's dollars after forty years of drawing. From one end of the dial to the other it moves by 0.7 pp/yr; the 5th-percentile path CAGR moves by 0.7 pp/yr the other way; volatility drops from 13 to 9. The ballast end holds JEPI, whose upside is contractually capped, and is reported as the boundary rather than a candidate.

## Where the survivors sit against what came before
The starting book P1 was 4.62 × and 3.90 %/yr. Every survivor beats it, by between 0.07 and 0.78 pp/yr, and P1 itself does not pass the concentration filter. The previous recommendation R7a (5.79 ×, 4.49 %/yr) sits just below the middle of the dial on wealth but fails the nuclear cap at 40%; four of the survivors beat it on wealth with less concentration and lower fees.

Two things separate the top of the dial from R7a: FNV 5 replaces filler, and DBMF is cut from 12–15 to 5–9. The first is well-supported — FNV has 18.7 years of history, significant alpha against equities, and a deck row that sits about 3.4 pp below its own honest construction, the one single-name row in the deck that is under-claimed rather than over-claimed. The second is the decision the model is least qualified to make: the engine prices managed futures through one fixed covariance and cannot see their tendency to be most negatively correlated to equities in the drawdowns that matter for a decumulator. That is why the middle of the dial, where DBMF is restored, is the author's preference.

## What the numbers are made of
The raw return behind the thematic end is roughly 8.8% nominal gross, and behind the middle roughly 8.5%. Subtract 0.4 for expense ratios, 2.5 for inflation, 0.9 for the draw (not 3 — Social Security offsets three-quarters of it from year 11 and the guardrail cuts it when it would hurt), and 0.14 for tax, and the retained figure is the 4.4–4.7 %/yr shown. Fees cost three times what tax does. A 13% CAGR is the 99th percentile of the forty-year distribution for these books and clears in about one decade in ten; the last seven years, at 20–23% for every book here, were roughly a one-in-five-hundred decade on this deck.

## How much to trust the levels
The ranking inside the family is worth little: simulation noise is ±0.01 ×, and perturbing deck rows by the sizes this campaign has actually observed moves a book seven or eight places. The levels carry a known direction of error. Four rows have been audited against real evidence and all four came down (CEG −2.5 then −2.1 pp, URNM −0.6, RPRX −3.1); every 100 bp of across-the-board deck optimism costs about 1.0 pp of retained CAGR; the deck's market row at 7.55% nominal sits above Vanguard's June-2026 ten-year forecast of 4.2–6.2%, with the Shiller CAPE at 41. Every survivor also holds 21–34% in funds with under five years of history — NUKZ 31 months, SHLD 35 — which is precisely where the corrections have come from. Read 4.6 as a number that could be 3.6.

## What is settled and what is not
Settled: the core six holdings; FNV at 5; two single names now cost about a third of a point a year and the two-single survivors (C3_balK2, C2_XAR) sit at 4.34 %/yr; GRID has been climbed out of every constrained search, five times from five seeds; R5c, R5c2, R5b and R7a are withdrawn.

Not settled, and not the model's to settle: the point on the dial. F2_bal2 is the proposed book on the two ratified criteria (best surprise-adjusted CE in the campaign, 3.205 ×); X2_F2_DBMF15 is the same book with the ballast the engine cannot price put back, and is the author's preference; C5_bal is the reliability anchor; F3_xar is the maximum-wealth point. The open method item is a regime-conditional covariance for the ballast sleeves, which would let the engine price the decision it currently cannot.
